What ALS is

The Alternative Learning System gives learners who left school, or never started, a second chance at basic education. Its K to 12 curriculum is aligned with, but not identical to, the formal school curriculum, so the lessons fit adult lives and schedules.

Six learning strands

Communication Skills

English and Filipino.

Scientific Literacy and Critical Thinking

Thinking through problems the way science does.

Mathematical and Problem-Solving Skills

Numbers for everyday life and work.

Life and Career Skills

Preparing for work and running a household.

Understanding the Self and Society

Identity, community and citizenship.

Digital Citizenship

Using technology safely and responsibly.

The A&E Test

The Accreditation and Equivalency Test measures what you learned against the ALS curriculum. Passers receive a certificate bearing the DepEd seal that certifies them as comparable graduates of the formal school system, at the elementary or junior high school level.

The next test date is announced through a DepEd Memorandum. Results are published on deped.gov.ph by year and level.

After you pass

Senior High School

Enrol in Grade 11 at any public school, or a private school of your choice.

Skills training

Take technical-vocational courses toward a National Certificate.

College

Junior high level passers may qualify for higher education, under DO 27, s. 2018.

How to enrol

  1. Find your nearest ALS centre

    Ask at a Community Learning Center, a public school or your Schools Division Office.

  2. Talk to an ALS teacher

    They check where you are in your learning and plan sessions around your work and family.

  3. Learn at your own pace

    Work through the six strands with your teacher and learning modules.

  4. Take the A&E Test

    When you are ready, sit the test to earn your certificate.
