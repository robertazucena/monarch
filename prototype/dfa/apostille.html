Apostille your documents for use abroad

An apostille certifies that a Philippine public document is genuine, so it is accepted in every country that is party to the Apostille Convention.

Documents that can be apostilled

The DFA apostilles public documents issued in the Philippines. Foreign documents are apostilled in the country that issued them.

Civil registry

PSA birth, marriage and death certificates, and CENOMAR, on security paper.

Clearances

NBI clearance issued with a QR code or verified by the NBI.

School records

Diplomas and transcripts with a CAV from CHED, DepEd or TESDA.

Notarised documents

Affidavits and SPAs with a certificate from the Regional Trial Court.

Court and government records

Court decisions and documents certified by the issuing office.

Business documents

SEC and DTI certificates, certified by the issuing agency.

Before you apply

Verification comes first. Many documents must be verified or certified by the agency that issued them before the DFA can apostille them. Start here to avoid a wasted trip.

What to bring

  • Confirmed apostille appointment slip
  • The original document, verified where needed
  • One valid government-issued ID of the applicant
  • If someone files for you: a signed authorisation letter or SPA and a photocopy of the owner’s ID

How it works

  1. Book at apostille.gov.ph

    Choose an office that offers apostille services and the processing speed you need.

  2. Submit and pay

    Hand in your documents and pay ₱100 per document for regular or ₱200 for expedited processing.

  3. Claim your documents

    Regular processing is released after 5 working days, expedited after 2. Courier delivery is available.

Countries that do not accept apostilles

If the destination country is not party to the Apostille Convention, the DFA still authenticates the document. You then take it to that country’s embassy in Manila for legalisation. Ask the embassy for its own requirements and fees.
