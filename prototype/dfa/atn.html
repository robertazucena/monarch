What the DFA can and cannot do

Assistance to Nationals is free. It is led by the Office of the Undersecretary for Migrant Workers’ Affairs (OUMWA) and every Philippine post abroad.

We can

  • Visit you in detention and make sure you have legal counsel
  • Help in accidents, serious illness and death, including repatriation of remains
  • Rescue and shelter victims of trafficking and abuse
  • Repatriate Filipinos in a crisis or conflict
  • Issue travel documents if your passport is lost

We cannot

  • Pay personal debts, fines or bail
  • Interfere in another country’s courts or laws
  • Act as your lawyer or travel agent
  • Find you a job or settle employment disputes on our own; we work with the Department of Migrant Workers

In an emergency

  1. Get to safety

    Call the local emergency number first if you are in immediate danger.

  2. Contact the nearest Philippine post

    Every embassy and consulate general keeps a 24-hour duty officer for emergencies.

  3. Give clear details

    Your full name, passport number, exact location, phone number and what has happened.

  4. Tell your family

    Ask a relative in the Philippines to call the ATN hotline too, so both sides can follow up.

Crisis alert levels

The DFA raises an alert level for a country when Filipinos there are at risk.

  1. Alert Level 1Precautionary

    Take precautions and stay informed through the Philippine post.

  2. Alert Level 2Restriction

    Limit non-essential movement. No new deployment of workers.

  3. Alert Level 3Voluntary repatriation

    The government offers flights home to those who want to leave.

  4. Alert Level 4Mandatory repatriation

    Everyone is asked to leave, with the government arranging travel.

Register with your post

If you live or work abroad, register with the Philippine Embassy or Consulate General that covers your city. In a crisis the post can then find you, contact you and include you in evacuation plans.
