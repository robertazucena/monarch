Our mandate

The Department advises the President on foreign policy and carries it out. It represents the Philippines in other countries and in international organisations, negotiates treaties, and delivers consular services to Filipinos at home and abroad.

Its work rests on the Constitution’s call for an independent foreign policy that puts national sovereignty, territorial integrity, national interest and the right to self-determination first.

The three pillars of foreign policy

Set out in the Philippine Foreign Service Act of 1991 (Republic Act 7157).

National security

Preserving and enhancing the country’s security, sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Economic security

Promoting trade, investment, tourism and development cooperation.

Filipinos overseas

Protecting the rights and promoting the welfare of Filipinos abroad.

History

  1. 1898
    The Department is founded

    The revolutionary government creates a Department of Foreign Affairs on 23 June, with Apolinario Mabini as its first Secretary.

  2. 1945
    Founding member of the United Nations

    The Philippines signs the UN Charter in San Francisco.

  3. 1946
    Independence

    The Department is re-established after the Philippines becomes independent on 4 July.

  4. 1967
    Founding member of ASEAN

    The Philippines joins Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand in signing the Bangkok Declaration.

  5. 1991
    Philippine Foreign Service Act

    Republic Act 7157 sets the three pillars of foreign policy and reorganises the foreign service.

  6. 1995
    Migrant Workers Act

    Republic Act 8042 strengthens protection and legal help for Filipinos working abroad.

  7. 2009
    The ePassport

    The Philippines begins issuing passports with an electronic chip.

  8. 2019
    Apostille Convention

    Philippine documents are authenticated with an apostille instead of the red ribbon.

  9. 2026
    ASEAN Chairmanship

    The Philippines chairs ASEAN and hosts its summits.

Organisation

The Department is organised into offices at its home office in Pasay City and posts abroad.

  • Office of the SecretaryLeads the Department and advises the President on foreign policy.
  • Office of Consular AffairsPassports, apostille and consular services in the Philippines.
  • Office of the Undersecretary for Migrant Workers’ AffairsAssistance to Nationals and the protection of Filipinos abroad.
  • Office of Treaties and Legal AffairsTreaties, international law and legal advice.
  • Regional and multilateral officesAsia and the Pacific, ASEAN, the Americas, Europe, the Middle East and Africa, and the United Nations.
  • Foreign Service InstituteTraining and research for the foreign service; an attached agency.
  • Technical Cooperation Council of the PhilippinesCoordinates technical cooperation with other countries; an attached agency.
  • UNESCO National Commission of the PhilippinesLinks the country with UNESCO programmes; an attached agency.
