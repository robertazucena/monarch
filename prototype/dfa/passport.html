Overview

Appointments are free. The DFA never sells slots through agents, Facebook pages or travel agencies. Report anyone who does.

A Philippine ePassport is issued to Filipino citizens. Adults receive a passport valid for ten years; applicants under 18 receive one valid for five years.

Bring the original of every document and one photocopy. Documents in a language other than English or Filipino need an official translation.

First-time adult applicants

Applying for the first time, aged 18 or over.

  • Confirmed online appointment and printed application form
  • PSA birth certificate on security paper, original and photocopy
  • One valid government-issued ID with photo, such as a PhilID or ePhilID, driver’s licence, UMID or PRC ID
  • Married women using their husband’s surname: PSA marriage certificate
  • If your birth was registered late: supporting documents showing your name and date of birth, such as school records

Renewing an ePassport

Your passport is still with you and the details have not changed.

  • Confirmed online appointment and printed application form
  • Your current ePassport and a photocopy of its data page
  • If you are changing your name or civil status: the PSA document that supports the change

Renewing a green non-machine-readable passport or a passport issued more than ten years ago? Bring the same documents as a first-time applicant.

Minors (under 18)

Minors receive a five-year passport. Fees are the same as for adults.

  • Personal appearance of the minor and a parent or legal guardian
  • PSA birth certificate of the minor
  • PSA marriage certificate of the parents, if married
  • Valid passport or government-issued ID of the parent who appears
  • If neither parent can appear: a notarised authorisation or SPA, the authorised person’s ID and, where needed, a DSWD travel clearance

Children 7 years old and below and their parents may use the Courtesy Lane without an appointment.

Lost or damaged passports

Report a lost passport as soon as possible so it cannot be misused.

  • Notarised affidavit of loss, or affidavit of mutilation for a damaged passport
  • Police report, if the lost passport was still valid
  • PSA birth certificate and one valid government-issued ID
  • The damaged passport itself, if you still have it

How it works

  1. Book a slot at passport.gov.ph

    Choose a consular office, date and time. You receive a confirmation and a reference number by email.

  2. Prepare your documents

    Print the application form and gather originals and photocopies of everything on the list.

  3. Appear in person

    Your documents are checked, your photo, fingerprints and signature are taken, and you pay the fee.

  4. Claim or receive your passport

    Claim it at the office with your official receipt, or have it delivered if you chose courier delivery.
