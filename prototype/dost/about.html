Our mandate

Provide central direction, leadership and coordination of scientific and technological efforts and ensure that the results therefrom are geared and utilized in areas of maximum economic and social benefits for the people.

Mission and vision

Vision

DOST as the leading enabler and provider of science, technology, and innovation (STI) explicit solutions towards national development.

Mission: to direct, lead, and coordinate the country’s scientific, technological, and innovative efforts geared towards maximum economic and social benefits for the people.

Desired outcomes

All programs, projects and activities of the Department work toward seven outcomes.

  1. Innovation stimulated
  2. Technology adoption promoted and accelerated
  3. A critical mass of globally competitive STI human resources developed
  4. Productivity and efficiency of communities and the production sector, particularly MSMEs, improved
  5. Resiliency to disaster risks and climate change ensured
  6. Inequality in STI capacities and opportunities reduced
  7. Effective STI governance achieved

History

  1. 1958
    National Science Development Board

    Congress establishes the NSDB on 13 June 1958, on the recommendation of Dr. Frank Co Tui, who surveyed the state of Philippine science and technology for the Garcia administration.

  2. 1981
    National Science and Technology Authority

    The NSDB is reconstituted as the NSTA on 17 March 1981, with broader policy-making and program-implementing functions, and the vision of “a competent and competitive science and technology community with a social conscience.”

  3. 1987
    The Department of Science and Technology

    Executive Order 128, signed on 30 January 1987, raises the NSTA to Cabinet level as the DOST.

With Cabinet stature, DOST took on three added duties: support local scientific and technological effort, build local capability for technological self-reliance, and encourage greater private-sector participation in research and development.

The DOST logo

Four circles joined side by side form a square. The circles stand for unit particles, the building blocks of nature and the subject of science and technology; their design gives an illusion of movement, signifying progress. The space at the centre forms a four-pointed star for scientific creativity.

The three colours stand for the unknown (black), truth and enlightenment (white) and progress (blue).
